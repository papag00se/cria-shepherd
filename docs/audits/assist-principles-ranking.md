# Assist ranking against the design principles

## Scope and interpretation

Assessed revision: **`079116e4c99635722a0f3327246fd9e359586eb5`**. Every source location below refers to that commit, not subsequent fixes or the shared checkout's in-flight changes. Read a cited location with `git show 079116e4:path`.

This ranks **all 78 registered atomic definitions** assembled by `cria/assistruntime.py:21–30`, against [the principles](../principles.md). It is not a claim that those definitions exhaust every supporting mechanism: the inventory also contains required foundations, protocol support, providers and external artifacts. Shared foundation qualifications follow the ranking.

Implementation, production callers, prompts and relevant test bodies were inspected. **Tests were read, not run. No live-capture walk, benefit experiment or behavioral-frequency measurement was performed.** A source-derived counterexample is not an observed campaign incident. Multiple assists sharing one defect are not independent incidents.

Ranks are tied tiers, not invented compliance percentages:

| Rank | Meaning | Definitions |
|---|---|---:|
| **1 — Aligned in inspected behavior** | No concrete contradiction found in the inspected scope. Not certification or demonstrated usefulness. | 20 |
| **2 — Qualified** | A scoped exception or credible unresolved tension; insufficient evidence for the stronger contradiction claim. | 6 |
| **3 — Prompt-location conflict** | Concrete §22 violation, without a separate established behavioral contradiction in this assessment. | 4 |
| **4 — Behavioral conflict** | A reachable implementation or prompt contradicts a substantive principle. | 48 |

Within each tier, registry order is retained; it is **not** a finer ranking. Rank 4 does not mean every part of an assist is wrong or that removing it wholesale is safe. Ground-truth, information-preservation and fail-safe conflicts take precedence over prompt placement, following the doctrine's explicit precedence.

**Retention remains unproven:** §1 requires current counterfactual benefit evidence. This assessment supplies none, including for rank 1. It also does not manufacture a universal violation merely because a new ablation was not performed here.

**Current profile boundary (operator decision): assists are L5 only.** This historical ranking includes definitions at every engagement level; its 48 Rank-4 rows remain unchanged. The `rank4-ablation` profile now disables only the nine Rank-4 definitions with `default_level = 5` (`cria/assistprofiles.py::RANK4_ABLATION_DISABLED`). The other 39 are L0–L4 wire fixes, cria tools, context fitting (including `rolling-transcript-memory` at L4 and `harness-memory-handoff` at L3), or completion brakes, so an assist-only ablation must retain them when level-eligible. The original all-48 selection incorrectly removed those mechanisms. `RANK4_DISABLED` preserves that ranking record, not the active profile. Historical runs keep their original identity and captures; compare their deployed revision and recorded `pilot_assists`, not the unchanged profile name alone. This policy correction does not resolve the source conflicts recorded below or establish benefit.

## Shared foundations and scope limits

Required/supporting mechanisms must not be silently counted as extra optional assists, nor assumed compliant because they are mandatory:

- **F68, final-wire context fitting:** one indivisible required contract when activated; activation is owned by `Upstream._prep`, preserving lower-level pass-through and unknown-window behavior. `cria/contextfloor.py` is explicitly lossless-first, not universally lossless. Description compression and labelled output/history reductions are pressure-boundary mechanisms, not permission for independent assists to clip. See `tests/test_wire_fit_foundation_contract.py:1–108` and `cria/upstream.py:557–571` for actual measured overflow refusal.
- **A concrete foundation qualification:** `cria/contextfloor.py:1023–1042` authors model-facing prose inline (§22), and calls historically modified files “still on disk” without a current existence check (§5b). Historical modification alone does not establish present existence. This is outside the 78 optional rankings, not an excuse to certify the foundation.
- **Wire ownership has genuine aligned examples:** role merging is last-mile and opt-in; private body hints are centrally stripped; historical argument repair follows message transforms. `cria/upstream.py:512–550`; `cria/bodykeys.py:1–25`. This establishes placement, not universal preservation of every upstream transform.
- **Workspace observations are not local-disk guesses by definition:** `cria/wsview.py:333–340,356–375` retains unknown existence and distinguishes current-survey bodies. Individual consumers can still overclaim; those are ranked above.
- **Planning remains OFF without explicit consent.** Optional planning ranks do not imply normal activation or authorize enabling it. `cria/assistruntime.py:275–282,381–387`; `cria/config.py:551–561`.
- **Supporting/provider/artifact coverage is not exhaustive here.** The 63 maintenance-family inventory contains mechanisms outside the 78-definition ranking. Their existence does not constitute inspected compliance or measured benefit.

## Bottom line

The cleanest inspected patterns are **schema-grounded adaptation, exact-copy pointers, current-survey restoration, explicit evidence provenance, focused positive-token judgments, and typed transport recovery**.

The largest departures are **lossy reduction disguised as repair/deduplication, partial evidence promoted to world facts, semantic vetoes made lexically, and deterministic fallback intervention after an unanswered judge**. Several tests deliberately preserve these behaviors: a passing regression suite would not by itself establish doctrinal compliance.

This is a ranking, not a removal or implementation plan. No runtime behavior, configured role, service, model or benchmark was changed for it.
