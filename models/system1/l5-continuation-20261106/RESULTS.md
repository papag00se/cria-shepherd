# System 1 continuation 001 — measured result

The original expanded-v2 native reference was continued on CPU for one epoch with all 836 prior training examples and 135 non-ambiguous November real calls. The run performed 61 accumulated updates in 10,053.6 seconds; settings and update events are in `cpu-fit-001/`. The new real inputs were grouped by source session before training or evaluation, with exact family/state/policy deduplication across the retained sets. Two complete-input retries were consolidated. No ambiguous or reserved case was used for training. Reviewer rationales, native predictions, confidence, audit outcomes and private reasoning were not used as targets or model inputs. The labels are assistant-reviewed adjudications, not human gold.

Both original and candidate were run on the exact same 364 complete inputs. The original native checkpoint and deployed INT4 bundle were evaluated independently from the new native checkpoint and its exported INT4 bundle. Each call verified identity of the captured token IDs, native preparation, and runtime preparation. `baseline-001/results.jsonl`, `candidate-eval-001/results.jsonl`, and `comparison.json` retain complete inputs, per-call answers, logits, timing and exposure scope. Counting below uses the actual INT4 runtime choices and deduplicated cases; the unit is a retained call input, not an independent task or session.

- **Reserved real, non-ambiguous:** original 20/21, candidate 20/21, all 21 decisions identical. Truth by prediction for both: PLAN→PLAN 4; RETROSPECTIVE→RETROSPECTIVE 16; RETROSPECTIVE→PLAN 1. There were no missed PLAN labels in these 21 cases. The set draws from only two sessions and is too small and clustered to establish general improvement. It was held out of this continuation, but its collection was previously analyzed; it is not human gold.
- **November exposed training replay:** original 113/135, candidate 123/135. Original confusion: PLAN→PLAN 21, PLAN→RETROSPECTIVE 19, RETROSPECTIVE→RETROSPECTIVE 92, RETROSPECTIVE→PLAN 3. Candidate confusion: 30, 10, 93 and 2 respectively. Eleven original errors changed to the adjudicated label and one correct original decision became an error. This is training replay, not independent accuracy.
- **Ambiguity:** on 13 ambiguous cases from training-side sessions, neither model was trained on their labels: original 5/13, candidate 4/13 against the provisional adjudications. One decision changed, C014 from RETROSPECTIVE to PLAN. Both original independent reviewers selected PLAN while the parent selected RETROSPECTIVE with explicit ambiguity. The one ambiguous case in a reserved session was PLAN from both, against its provisional RETROSPECTIVE adjudication. These are policy-sensitive analyses, not certain hard ground truth.
- **Earlier synthetic control:** original and candidate each 64/64, equally split across both families. These are assistant-reviewed synthetic paired scenarios, not fresh real cases. The candidate export also used these inputs to construct runtime self-test cases; no labels were used to change the weights.
- **Earlier exposed real replay:** original and candidate each 130/130 (112 retrospective, 18 dictation). These were already included in expanded-v2 training and are not held out.

The fixed split explains an important measurement limitation: its two reserved sessions contain 17 RETROSPECTIVE and 4 PLAN labels, and the original INT4 model already returned PLAN for all four PLAN cases. It cannot measure whether the candidate corrects missed PLAN cases in new sessions. The training side contains 95 RETROSPECTIVE and 40 PLAN cases across 13 sessions; one session contributes 34 cases. Exact deduplication does not make similar successive briefings independent. Four of the 11 replay corrections (C016–C019) come from one Java session and share substantially similar briefing content, including the named-library instruction. This is observed concentration, not proof of why the fit changed. The next concrete source question is whether independently reviewed, distinct-session examples of task restatement versus newly prescribed repair—and copied guidance versus reported prior intent—show the same tradeoff without overrepresenting successive revisions. Gather those from other real sessions, retain full task and policy context for labeling where available, keep unresolved interpretations out of hard targets, and reserve different sessions for any subsequent evaluation. Another fit cannot resolve this evidence gap by tuning against either existing reserved set.

The one newly incorrect non-ambiguous exposed decision is C090: both original reviewers called its shipping-requirement restatement RETROSPECTIVE, while the candidate calls it PLAN. The complete policy and classifier input are retained in `train.json` and the paired evaluations; the original capture was read in full but is not published. This is a potential false-rejection tradeoff at the task-restatement boundary, not a measured broader rate. The reserved comparison did not change at all, so it does not establish that the improvement on exposed cases transfers. New data/source diagnosis and an independent review of the tradeoff are warranted. The operator subsequently directed deployment despite the mixed evidence; replay gains alone did not establish general improvement.

## Export, fidelity and constraints

The native checkpoint and tokenizer are in `cpu-fit-001/reference`; the quantization recipe and report are in `cpu-fit-001/export-001`. The serving INT4 graph and external weights are retained once, in `cria/system1_bundle`. The large intermediate floating-point ONNX graph and duplicate local package are not committed; they can be regenerated as described in the adjacent README. The actual INT4 graph plus external weight data are 239,829,460 bytes. On all 364 evaluated full inputs, candidate native FP32 logits and the normalized FP32 ONNX graph passed `rtol=atol=0.0002`; maximum absolute logit difference was 0.0000692. Native and INT4 choices differed on 1/364 candidate inputs and 7/364 original inputs, all in the exposed November replay. The evaluation's maximum observed input length was 3,197 tokens; this is not evidence of an actual inference at the full declared 16,384-token window.

The packaged runtime self-test passed for both deployed families. `window-check.json` records both families refusing an over-budget full input before inference and refusing an over-window input at preparation, without clipping: this check used an 8 GiB arena, whose measured preflight ceiling is 4,714 tokens. Production's separate 16 GiB arena and 16,384-token nominal window remain unchanged; longer real cases were not run here.

Evaluation ran with two Torch/runtime CPU threads, low priority and shared host contention; native FP32 export inference also used the owner's six-thread ORT session. This is not an isolated speed benchmark. Candidate INT4 per-call time for 50 dictation inputs was mean 2.54 s, median 2.42 s, p95 3.34 s; for 314 retrospective inputs mean 3.98 s, median 3.26 s, p95 8.66 s. Peak process RSS was 13,499,052 KiB for the combined candidate native + FP32 ONNX + INT4 evaluation, not standalone runtime memory. Original combined native + INT4 peak RSS was 6,825,784 KiB, not a like-for-like memory comparison.

## Additional fresh real-call diagnostic

After the first reserved comparison showed no changed decisions, a deterministic, outcome-blind sample of 32 earlier November 2–5 suite sessions was selected, one retained call per session, excluding exact inputs in either original cohort or the November 6 collection, and excluding November 6 sessions. Selection occurred after the first comparison but before the independent labels or paired predictions of this sample were inspected. These 32 inputs were not used to train either checkpoint. A single assistant reviewer judged complete policy and input without seeing provenance or predictions; the review is not human gold. Same-task sessions can still be related and the sample is not representative by construction. The blind inputs, original records, independent review and paired runtime outputs remain local under `fresh-real-review/`; these private source/reviewer records are not published, so this diagnostic is not independently reproducible from the committed corpus.

On 29 non-ambiguous inputs, original INT4 matched 22 and candidate 23. Original confusion: PLAN→PLAN 5, PLAN→RETROSPECTIVE 6, RETROSPECTIVE→RETROSPECTIVE 17, RETROSPECTIVE→PLAN 1. Candidate: 6, 5, 17 and 1 respectively. F026 and F027 changed from missed PLAN to PLAN, including copied future-facing guidance; F006 changed from correct PLAN to a missed PLAN, concerning a proposed migration approach. Thus two errors were corrected and one introduced on this small selected sample; this is a local tradeoff, not a general accuracy estimate. All three complete inputs and policies were inspected.

The remaining three reviewer-marked uncertain inputs are separate. Against the reviewer's original blind semantic labels, original matched 3/3, candidate 2/3; F002 changed from PLAN to RETROSPECTIVE. The reviewer later noted that the captured policy says to answer PLAN when uncertain, and recorded post-prediction operational PLAN corrections for F016 and F029. That correction is retained but is not a blinded amendment or a license to silently hard-label uncertain examples. Against those operational labels the original returned PLAN on 1/3 and the candidate on 0/3. The 32-call diagnostic measures only retrospective classification; prior dictation controls are reported separately above.

After this evaluation the operator directed deployment of the candidate; see [the deployment report](../../../docs/training/system1-l5-continuation.md). The 21-case reserved set and this additional diagnostic cannot be reused as unexposed benchmarks for another fit if their labels are used to guide that fit. Any additional candidate selection requires fresh session-separated evaluation material; retain all exposure histories.
